Southampton to Agincourt, 1415

Until Crispin's Day

It is August 1415. The King is taking an army to France, and you have taken his sixpence.

You are a cordwainer's apprentice from Winchester with a borrowed bow, the twentieth man in a company of twenty archers. Your vintenar is Hob Cutler. He has taken men over the water before, and he does not expect much of you.

How it begins

The quay stinks of tar and horse and the breath of twelve thousand men waiting on a wind. You have a borrowed bow, a blister where the string bites, and no idea where you are meant to stand.

Vintenar Hob Cutler finds you before you find him. He looks you over the way a fishmonger looks at a herring he has been asked to take back. 'Name on the roll, stand where I can see you, and try not to die before we sail,' he says. 'It upsets the clerks.'

What you do

You read a scene and pick what happens next. Some choices are safe. Others are a gamble, and the game gives you the odds in words, anywhere from near certain down to a prayer. What you choose decides what Cutler makes of you, and whether you are still on your feet a week later.

It saves after every choice, so ten minutes is enough to get somewhere.

The campaign is the real one. The army marched out of Harfleur for Calais with eight days' food, and it rained all night before the battle. The archer and the men around him are made up.

It is a war story, told with a straight face and the odd joke at the worst possible moment. People die in it.

What this site records

Your saves are kept in this browser. There is no account.

The game sends me the name you give your archer, how far he got and which choices were made, so I can see where people get stuck. It does not know who you are. Google Analytics counts visits, and sets cookies to do it.
